# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

We want this project to be a constructive place for technical, legal-research, and architecture work.

Contributors and participants are expected to:

- discuss ideas and evidence rather than attack people;
- challenge architecture, legal reasoning, and implementation directly but respectfully;
- distinguish disagreement from misconduct;
- disclose uncertainty and limitations when they matter;
- avoid presenting speculation as fact;
- respect privacy, confidentiality, and intellectual-property boundaries;
- keep review feedback focused on the work and its consequences.

Adversarial review is welcome. Hostility is not required for rigor.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or discriminatory abuse;
- personal attacks unrelated to the work;
- deliberate disclosure of another person's private information;
- posting confidential legal/business material without authorization;
- knowingly misrepresenting evidence or review results;
- repeated disruptive behavior after a maintainer asks for it to stop.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and other project spaces controlled by the maintainers.

## Reporting conduct problems

Use GitHub's reporting/moderation tools where appropriate.

For a matter that should not be handled publicly, contact the maintainer through the GitHub account associated with this repository and avoid posting sensitive details in a public issue.

Maintainers may edit, hide, lock, or remove content and may restrict participation when necessary to protect the project or its participants.

## Review culture

Strong technical disagreement is allowed and expected.

A reviewer may conclude that a proposal is wrong, unsafe, unsupported, architecturally unsound, or not ready to merge. That is not misconduct when the criticism is directed at the work and supported by reasons or evidence.
