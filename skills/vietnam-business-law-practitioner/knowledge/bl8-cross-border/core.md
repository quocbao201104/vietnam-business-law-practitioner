# BL8 — Investment / Cross-border / Trade

## Owns when acting as proposition owner

BL8 is the accountable owner for material propositions about:

- foreign-investor status;
- foreign-investment market access and foreign-investment control tests;
- governing-law/conflict-of-laws propositions;
- treaty/CISG applicability;
- FX/cross-border payment classification;
- inbound/outbound investment overlay;
- goods-trade/customs propositions;
- specialist trade/origin activation and owner integration.

## Overlay role

BL8 may also operate only as a cross-border overlay when the substantive proposition remains owned elsewhere.

Examples:

- BL2 owns corporate cap table, voting/control, and approvals; BL8 owns foreign-investment control tests and market-access consequences.
- BL3 owns contract formation/content and the existence/content of a dispute-resolution clause; BL8 owns cross-border governing-law/treaty/international-enforcement overlay.
- BL4 owns dispute posture, invocation/procedure, deadlines, and remedies under the already resolved forum/regime.
- BL5 owns tax consequences; BL8 may classify the foreign payment/investment/trade flow that BL5 consumes.
- BL7 owns domestic market/product permission; BL8 owns border/trade propositions.

## Does not own

Corporate mechanics (BL2), ordinary contract obligations (BL3), breach/remedies/procedure (BL4), tax treatment (BL5), domestic product/market permission (BL7).

## Activate when

A party, owner/investor, performance location, asset, payment, governing-law clause, dispute-enforcement context, or goods movement has a foreign element that can materially change a proposition.

## Core distinctions

- cross-border ≠ import/export;
- corporate validity ≠ foreign-investment compliance;
- corporate control (BL2) ≠ foreign-investment control test (BL8);
- ownership percentage ≠ complete market-access analysis;
- governing law ≠ dispute clause content ≠ dispute procedure ≠ international enforcement;
- international sale ≠ automatic application of Vietnamese commercial law;
- foreign invoice payment ≠ foreign loan ≠ capital contribution;
- product name ≠ HS classification;
- classification ≠ origin ≠ preferential tariff entitlement;
- FTA exists ≠ zero tariff;
- Incoterm ≠ complete legal allocation;
- contractual import-cost allocation ≠ statutory customs responsibility.

## Decision procedure

1. Map the foreign element and classify the cross-border mode: investment, contract/service, payment/loan, dispute/enforcement, or goods trade.
2. Decide whether BL8 is the accountable owner for a cross-border proposition or only an overlay to another owner.
3. For investment, resolve foreign-investor/market-access/control-test propositions and return corporate mechanics to BL2.
4. For contracts, resolve governing law/treaty/CISG propositions where material; BL3 then reasons about formation/obligations under the resolved regime.
5. For dispute clauses, BL3 records clause content; BL8 resolves cross-border conflict/treaty/international-enforcement overlay; BL4 handles invocation/procedure/remedies.
6. For payments, classify current/capital/loan/investment flow and coordinate tax consequence with BL5.
7. For goods, activate trade-policy → HS → origin → valuation/tariff → customs chain only as needed; invoke technical specialists under BL8 ownership.
8. Send domestic product/market permission to BL7.
9. Resolve authority using proposition-specific temporal anchors such as closing, payment, border entry, or enforcement date.

## Specialist rule

HS/origin/customs specialists return candidate technical findings to BL8. BL8 decides whether to accept/condition/reject and only then updates owned propositions.

## Failure modes

- foreign party treated as import/export;
- BL8 treated as foreign-case super-owner;
- Vietnam party treated as automatic Vietnamese governing law;
- BL4 and BL8 independently resolving forum/procedure;
- BL2 corporate control reused as foreign-investment control without separate test;
- FTA-country shipment treated as preferential origin;
- HS guessed from name;
- DDP treated as statutory responsibility transfer;
- foreign ownership percentage used as sole access test;
- future law treated as current;
- specialist finding bypassing BL8 owner review.
