# Search Strategy

Search is a designed runtime dependency for volatile legal authority. It is not required for every conceptual question.

## Search when the proposition is materially time-sensitive or action-facing

Typical triggers:

- current rights or obligations;
- validity/enforceability;
- rates, thresholds, deadlines, fines, forms, filing mechanics;
- licensing or market-entry requirements;
- tax treatment;
- current procedure;
- historical transactions governed by prior law;
- foreign/treaty applicability;
- conflicting authority;
- recently changing regulation.

## Search sequence

1. Define the exact proposition requiring verification.
2. Identify the relevant date/event.
3. Identify jurisdiction and likely authority type.
4. Locate the highest appropriate official source.
5. Check effective date and lifecycle status.
6. Check amendment, replacement, suspension, consolidation, or transition rules.
7. Read the controlling passage in context.
8. Use official guidance/practice/secondary sources only as needed to interpret, challenge, or discover further authority.
9. Store the resolved proposition and authority in shared state so other tracks can reuse it.

## Temporal checks

Do not equate publication with effectiveness.

Distinguish at least:

- draft/consultation;
- promulgated but future-effective;
- currently binding;
- historical version;
- amended;
- superseded/repealed;
- suspended;
- uncertain/transitioning.

For future planned actions, verify whether an enacted future rule will become applicable by the planned action date.

## Historical questions

For a past transaction/event, search for the law applicable to the relevant event date rather than simply using the current consolidated text.

Check transition provisions when a relationship spans multiple legal regimes.

## Search terms

Search by legal proposition and transaction facts, not only by the user's noun label.

Examples:

Bad: `freelancer tax Vietnam`

Better: identify employment-vs-service classification first, then search the current tax/social-insurance consequences of the resolved category.

Bad: `FTA Korea 0%`

Better: identify product classification/origin issue, relevant FTA, current tariff year, and origin requirement.

## Conflicting sources

When sources conflict:

1. compare authority level;
2. compare effective period;
3. compare scope and factual applicability;
4. check whether one source has been amended/replaced;
5. preserve ambiguity if conflict remains material.

Do not resolve conflict by citation count.

## Search stop condition

Stop when there is enough current authoritative evidence to support the material proposition at the level needed for the decision.

Do not continue collecting sources merely to make the answer look researched.
