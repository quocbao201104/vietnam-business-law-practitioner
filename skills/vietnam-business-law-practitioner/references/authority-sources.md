# Authority Sources

Use source authority appropriate to the proposition being verified. This file is a source-routing guide, not a static legal database.

## Primary and official sources

Prefer current official text and official repositories for binding legal propositions.

Typical source families include:

- Vietnam legal-document databases maintained by competent state authorities;
- Government legal-document publication portals;
- ministry/regulator legal-document portals;
- judiciary/precedent portals where judicial authority is material;
- official treaty/FTA texts and official trade portals;
- official corporate-registration, tax, customs, labour/social-insurance, investment, and competition authorities when the issue falls within their competence.

## Official guidance

Official Q&A, administrative guidance, implementation notices, and regulator explanations can be strong evidence of administrative practice or interpretation.

Do not silently promote guidance into legislation. Check whether the document is legally binding, merely interpretive, transaction-specific, or local/administrative.

## Judicial and dispute-practice material

Use applicable precedents, judicial instruments, and authoritative court materials according to their actual legal status.

Arbitral awards/practitioner case summaries may illuminate contract and dispute reasoning but are not automatically binding precedent.

## Practitioner and institutional material

Useful sources can include business-association commentary, arbitration institutions, professional materials, and law-firm analysis.

Use them to:

- discover issues;
- understand business impact;
- find primary authority;
- surface competing interpretations;
- learn practice patterns.

Do not use them as silent substitutes for available primary authority.

## Academic material

Academic and doctrinal sources are useful for:

- unresolved distinctions;
- legal doctrine;
- empirical court/practice research;
- competing interpretation;
- comparative or theoretical analysis.

They are reasoning inputs, not automatically binding authority.

## Legal-tech, repository, and AI material

Skills, repositories, retrieval systems, and AI-generated summaries are research leads or architecture inputs only unless they directly surface independently verifiable authority.

They must not become the final authority for a material proposition.

## Proposition-specific sufficiency

There is no universal source count.

A proposition may be sufficiently supported by one controlling current provision. Another may require statute + implementing regulation + authoritative interpretation.

Authority sufficiency depends on the proposition, legal status, temporal context, and ambiguity—not citation quantity.
