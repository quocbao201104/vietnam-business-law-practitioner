# Citation Guidance

Citation is evidence for a proposition, not decoration.

## Cite when

A material conclusion depends on current/historical legal authority, including rights, obligations, validity, remedy, tax treatment, threshold/rate, deadline, licensing, sanction, procedure, or current compliance.

## Do not impose a source quota

One controlling current provision can be stronger than several secondary citations.

Use as many sources as the proposition actually requires.

## Traceability

A material proposition should be traceable to:

- source identity;
- relevant provision/passage;
- authority type/status;
- relevant effective period where temporal application matters.

## Separate authority from interpretation

Do not make an interpretive conclusion look like quoted law.

Make clear when a proposition is:

- source text/rule;
- application to facts;
- derived legal interpretation;
- business recommendation.

## User/document facts

Do not cite externally for facts supplied by the user unless independently verifying them is itself material.

A statement in an uploaded document should be described as documented evidence/representation unless separately verified.
